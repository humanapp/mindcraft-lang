Wendoo - brand

Mark, logotype, color, type

Everything on this page is one color per placement. Knockouts are evenodd geometry. Grid: 24 units for the master mark, 16 units for the small cut. The SVG files in logo/ are the canonical assets.

01Mark

Master - 24 grid - use at 24px and above

Both silhouettes 10 x 10, gap 2, radius 2.8 outer and 0.8 inner. The solid tile is drawn to the hollow tile's outer edge, so WHEN and DO are equal weight. The pair spans 22 of 24.

Pupil r1.9, offset +0.55, at 0.46 of tile height. Clearance to the ring is 0.55 units, so the eye stays an eye down to roughly 24px. The same pupil is knocked out of DO, so fill is the only difference between the two eyes.

Stroke 2. It becomes the letter stroke of the logotype.

Small cut - 16 grid - use at 20px and below

Tiles 7.4, gap 1.2, stroke 1.8, pupil r1.1 offset +0.3. Pupil clearance is 0.5 of 16, which is half a pixel at 16px. Nothing below that survives rasterisation.

Size ladder - master at 96, 48, 32, 24; small cut at 20 and 16
App icon - same containment, dark ground

02Logotype

x-height equals tile height (10), letter stroke equals ring stroke (2), letter gap equals tile gap (2). Letter silhouettes are 10 wide, w is 14, so the tiles occupy exactly two letter slots. The viewBox is 78 x 19; at 28px tall it is 115px wide.

Tile-native

every counter is the tile - the wordmark
28px header
inverted

The d's bowl is the hollow tile with a stem, so the eye has already learned "rounded square = letter" by the time it reaches the oo, and reads them as letters without hesitation. Squared counters also sit well next to code and industrial UI. The tile radius 2.8 outer / 0.8 inner becomes the whole brand's corner language.

Round monoline

alternative construction, kept for comparison

Circles for e, d, n; the tiles stay squares. Friendlier and more obviously a word, but the circles and the squares are two vocabularies, and the d bowl and the WHEN tile argue about which is the letter.

03Color

The mark is always one color. Accents live in surfaces and UI. Ink and paper are toned, never pure. Two palettes are defined; neither has been chosen yet.

Blueprint

cool ink - ultramarine - one LED amber
Ink
oklch(0.20 0.015 260)
Paper

Ultramarine
oklch(0.50 0.20 265)
Amber, status only

Engineering drawings and the one amber LED on every microcontroller board. Ultramarine is the surface color (buttons, selection); amber is reserved for state (running, warning) and never decorates.

Fired clay tiles and a single hot indicator. Warm ink and paper feel like a printed workbook rather than a dashboard; one saturated magenta does all the accent work, so there is never a second color to misread as meaning.
